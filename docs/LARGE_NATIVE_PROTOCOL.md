## Data and acquisition

Official CDC BRFSS 2025 was released August 2026: 356,158 respondents and 284 published variables. The 2024 cohort has 457,670 respondents. We use 39 common documented covariates to retrospectively classify reported diabetes or lifetime asthma. The two targets share respondents and constitute one health domain. Exclude disease-specific follow-up, care, derived outcomes, identifiers, weights and asthma-current status. Age and BMI are demographic/anthropometric exceptions; the published age covariate is already imputed. Unknown/refused/structural blank fields remain missing; common codebooks preserve legitimate insurance/income codes 7/9 and explicitly map documented zero responses. No population prevalence or clinical effectiveness claim. State coverage and questionnaire specifications change between years; no person linkage across surveys is available.

Official UK DfT collision data have 100,927 records in 2024 and 101,525 in 2025. Final 2025 data were released July 30, 2026, with September revisions documented on the source page. Binary target is recorded fatal/serious versus slight collision severity. Twenty-three common context covariates exclude casualty/vehicle counts, severity adjustments, injury indicators, attendance, region/location IDs and road numbers. Documented unknown codes and -1 become missing; valid zero codes remain valid. Legacy and canonical junction/crossing/hazard fields coexist: reporting-specification migration produces substantial structural missingness and confounds causal interpretation. These are police-reported injury collisions, not all road events. OGL v3 attribution applies.

Sources: [CDC 2025](https://www.cdc.gov/brfss/annual_data/annual_2025.html), [CDC 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/brfss/annual_data/annual_2024.html), [DfT open data](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/road-safety-open-data). ## Fixed evaluation and controls

Three training seeds: 171001, 171002, 171003. Source uses 2024 states/police forces whose numeric group code modulo five is 0, 1 or 2. Validation uses 2025 groups with residue 3; development uses 2025 groups with residue 4. Metadata-only SHA256 ordering fixes row selection. Reserve 32 disjoint, within-group meta-training episodes, each with 128 support labels and 512 query labels. Then reserve up to 50,000 disjoint source backbone labels (minimum 16,384) and 128 source context labels. The actual count is reported. Within each eligible held-out group, reserve 128 support rows and at most 2,048 query rows; exclude groups with fewer than 640 eligible rows. All label-access and row boundaries are audited. Query outcomes enter training only in meta-training episodes; held-out query labels serve scoring only.

CUDA XGBoost uses 200 trees, depth 5, learning rate .05 and lambda 1, with coded covariates quantitative. Four nonconstant incomplete mask channels are selected by source-fit missing rates only. The existing 16-expert parity bank and 961-parameter scratch mixture are reused: this is a broader development evaluation of the current model, not a new architecture or novelty claim. Fixed 200 Adam epochs, batch 8, learning rate .001. Primary is native_scratch. Controls: matched-update linear scorer, float64 L-BFGS linear scorer (2,000 iteration limit, 1e-7 gradient tolerance), convex global simplex stack, global Platt, support-only ridge intercept/Platt, frozen source, CV selector and moment selector. Global controls receive the same meta-query labels. Convergence diagnostics are retained; finite optimization does not guarantee the best nonconvex solution. No development-based selection or stopping.

Report NLL for every model, task, seed and held-out group, plus group means and training variability. Three seeds share data and are not independent datasets. Only two domains are covered; health outcomes correlate. Any benefit must survive simple controls and source/reporting confounds. No confirmation, safety, clinical effectiveness, acceptance or venue-readiness claim. Existing panels and negative results remain unchanged.
