# Lifted cavity networks: results of protocols v2 and v3

The paper is `paper/lifted_cavity/main.pdf`. Every number below and in the paper is regenerated by
`experiments/lifted_cavity/make_paper.py` from the per-task scores in `artifacts/reports/lifted_cavity_paper/`.
Protocols: `LIFTED_CAVITY_PROTOCOL_V2.md` and `LIFTED_CAVITY_PROTOCOL_V3.md`, each committed before its panels existed.

## Protocol v2: five of six endpoints pass

| ID | Data | Comparison | Gain (nats) [95% CI] | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | F1, k=2 | lifted cavity network vs NL-FA | +0.054 [+0.043, +0.065] | pass |
| E2 | F1, k=2 | lifted cavity network vs transformer | +0.033 [+0.027, +0.040] | pass |
| E3a | F3 (16 sensors), k=2 | lifted cavity network vs FA-Gaussian | +0.148 [+0.115, +0.181] | pass |
| E3b | F3 (16 sensors), k=2 | lifted cavity network vs transformer | +0.144 [+0.127, +0.161] | pass |
| E4a | Beijing PM2.5 | fine-tuned lifted cavity network vs BLR | +0.194 [+0.131, +0.257] | pass |
| E4b | Beijing PM2.5 | fine-tuned lifted cavity network vs fine-tuned transformer | −0.118 [−0.145, −0.091] | **fail** |

**What holds.**
- On synthetic tasks, the 8k-parameter lifted cavity network beats the 203k-parameter TabPFN-v2-style transformer
  trained on the same prior at every number of missing sensors.
- It closes 58% of the gap between FA-Gaussian and the HMC Bayes-optimal predictive at k=2, against 34% for the
  transformer.
- It transfers from 5 to 16 sensors, where the transformer falls to the closed form's level.

**What fails.**
- On real networks after fine-tuning, the transformer is better: on Beijing PM2.5, and on Air Quality CO and NO2.
- Post-hoc diagnostics locate the gap in the Gaussian site family, which underfits real data:
  - a second nuisance dimension does not help;
  - five times longer fine-tuning helps only partly;
  - a per-episode GP is far worse, so the gap is not per-episode nonparametrics.

**Theory added in the paper.**
- Proposition 3: a product of static Gaussian sites needs a nuisance dimension at least the shared-noise rank.
- With Proposition 2, that rank is also sufficient.

## Protocol v3: lifted sites as the transformer's output layer

Hypothesis, formed after v2: the transformer backbone with a lifted-site output layer (the lifted cavity transformer,
LCT) keeps real-data flexibility and gains the lifted model's extrapolation. Endpoints E5–E7b are computed by
`confirm3.py` into `artifacts/reports/lifted_cavity_paper/runs/confirm_v3.json`.

| ID | Data | Comparison | Gain (nats) [95% CI] | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E5 | G1, k=2 | LCT vs transformer | +0.035 [+0.027, +0.042] | pass |
| E6 | G3 (16 sensors), k=2 | LCT vs transformer | +0.150 [+0.133, +0.168] | pass |
| E7a | Beijing NO2, natural | fine-tuned LCT vs fine-tuned lifted network | +0.062 [+0.024, +0.100] | pass |
| E7b | Beijing NO2, natural | fine-tuned LCT non-inferior to fine-tuned transformer (margin −0.02) | −0.021 [−0.093, +0.052] | **fail** |

**Reading.**
- Lifted sites as the output layer fix the transformer's synthetic weakness.
  - The LCT matches the small lifted network on five sensors and beats it on sixteen.
  - Its training NLL reaches the plain transformer's final value after about a third of training.
- On the new real target, the fine-tuned scores are: transformer 0.199, LCT 0.220, BLR 0.275, lifted network 0.282.
  - The LCT recovers 75% of the lifted network's gap to the transformer.
  - The LCT–transformer difference is not significant either way, but non-inferiority within 0.02 is not
    established.
  - With six more stations removed, the LCT and the transformer tie.
- Descriptive, on the protocol-v2 panels scored so far, with two missing sensors:
  - The LCT is within 0.005 nats of the lifted network or better.
  - It beats both comparators under strong nonlinearity (F4).

Post-hoc robustness runs (second transformer and LCT seeds, LCT on Beijing PM2.5) are listed in the paper's appendix
as they complete.
