# A learned uncertainty mixture: separately frozen developmental hypothesis

Motivation from failed context prototype: centered moments explain more signal while every aggressive correction can harm null tasks. Test whether a learned uncertainty mixture improves finite-support estimation beyond calibrated guards and equally pretrained linear weighting. Do not claim a new acquisition mechanism or theory contribution.

Keep the first pilot's generator and strong controls frozen. New seeds120000-120767/122000-122063/124000-124047 are disjoint from all earlier training/validation/development. The single-parity structural prior and sufficient-U limitation remain. This is an estimation hypothesis, not an expanded synthetic distribution engineered to defeat the comparator.

Sixteen experts comprise frozen prediction and fifteen target-support-fitted ridge parity corrections. Fits use64 labels only, with fixed ridge. Three-fold out-of-support NLL, residual moments, source agreement and fitted coefficients form12-dimensional candidate context. A shared32-to16 neural scorer learns mixture weights through empirical query BCE on768 paired-policy worlds/1536 episodes,60epochs. A linear scorer receives identical inputs, synthetic queries and updates; its capacity differs by design. Candidate probabilities are mixed, not logits added: uncertainty can reduce unsupported corrections. Weights are predictive mixture weights, not a calibrated Bayesian posterior.

Additional strong controls retain intercept/mask-CV/full-parity-CV/pooled-CV/moment and include OOF-CV expert selection and a moment-versus-frozen guard whose threshold is chosen from a fixed five-value grid using64 meta-validation worlds only. Neural/linear final checkpoints are fixed at epoch60. No evaluation query labels, true mechanism or oracle enter expert fitting, weighting, guard selection or inference. Source labels are available equally, though expert fitting uses target labels. Candidate fits, folds and costs are disclosed; matched update counts do not prove optimization fairness.
