Low-cost sensor deployments must estimate a target from whichever sensors work, after a short calibration, while
temperature, humidity and drift perturb several sensors at once. We cast this as in-context probabilistic prediction
that must operate under different missingness patterns and numbers of sensors. For linear-Gaussian sensors with shared
noise, we show that mask-independent scalar Gaussian experts cannot in general reproduce every pattern's posterior. We propose
\emph{lifted cavity networks}: each observed sensor contributes a rank-one Gaussian site over the target and a
low-dimensional shared nuisance, exact for factor-structured noise under every pattern once the nuisance dimension
reaches the noise rank; necessity holds for static linear-Gaussian sites under a generic-rank condition. The sites start from a factor analysis of the incomplete
calibration rows and are refined by a learned, cavity-conditioned re-linearization.
The same support-calibrated sites also serve as a learned transformer output layer.
