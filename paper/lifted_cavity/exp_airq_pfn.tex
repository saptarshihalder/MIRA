\paragraph{Why the transformer wins on real data.}
The transformer is better on \FTbeijingpfnbetterpct\% of Beijing test episodes, by
\FTbeijinggapqQOne--\FTbeijinggapqQTwo\ nats in each of the three easier quartiles of closed-form difficulty. The
lifted network underfits rather than overfits: the transformer's final training NLL is \FTbeijingtraingap\ nats
lower, about the test gap. Neither a second nuisance dimension (\Xplifttwo) nor five times more fine-tuning steps
(\Xpliftlong, against \Xpliftone; Table~\ref{tab:explore}) closes the gap to the transformer
(\RbeijingeZerovalftpfn), and per-episode nonparametrics do worse still ($k$-nearest neighbours \RbeijingeZerovalknn,
Gaussian process \RbeijingeZerovalgp). We therefore hypothesized that the limitation lies in how sites are refined
(each correction sees only its own sensor's reading, calibration scatter and cavity) rather than in the site family;
protocol v3 tests this.
