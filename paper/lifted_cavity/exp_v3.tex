\subsection{Lifted sites as a transformer output layer (protocol v3)}
\label{sec:exp-v3}

Protocol v2 left a split: structure wins on synthetic tasks and in extrapolation, while the transformer wins on real
networks after fine-tuning. Protocol v3 tests whether the two combine. The \emph{lifted cavity transformer} (LCT)
keeps the transformer's backbone and training recipe unchanged and replaces only its output head. A zero-initialized
linear map turns each observed query cell's final token into a correction
$(\delta^{\mathrm{off}}_j,\delta^{\mathrm{slope}}_j,\delta^{D}_j,s_j)$ of that sensor's lifted site, linearized at the
FA-Gaussian posterior mean, and the prediction is the $y$-marginal of the product of observed sites. Missing sensors
therefore contribute no site, the readings of missing sensors never enter, and at initialization the LCT is the
FA-Gaussian closed form of its anchor. The hypothesis was formed after seeing the v2 real-data results, so v3 uses
only new data: two fresh synthetic panels (G1 with five sensors, G3 with sixteen) and two Beijing pollutants that no
model had been scored on (NO$_2$ and CO, same stations and periods). Its four endpoints were committed before any of
these existed (Table~\ref{tab:endpoints}).
