\paragraph{Lifted sites as a transformer output layer.}\label{sec:method-lct}
The \emph{lifted cavity transformer} (LCT) uses the same cell-token backbone as the plain transformer, with a
zero-initialized linear map from each observed query-cell token to the site corrections in~\eqref{eq:relin}.
It linearizes once at the FA-Gaussian posterior mean, then returns the target marginal of the product of observed
sites. Unlike the small network, it does not perform three cavity-refinement rounds. The backbone can condition
site corrections on the full support and query subset; lifting constrains the probabilistic readout, not those
interactions. Missing readings are excluded and a zero head recovers the anchor, so parts (i) and (iii) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:struct} carry over; part (ii) is not asserted for the transformer's random column embeddings.
Given the tokens, site aggregation and the $(K+1)$-dimensional solve cost
$O(P(K+1)^2+(K+1)^3)$ per query; this excludes the backbone and support-anchor fitting.
The contrast with a plain transformer tests this readout as a package: it changes both the head parameterization
and initialization, without separately attributing their effects.
