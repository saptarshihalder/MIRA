\begin{abstract}
We study how a trained correction can adapt a frozen tabular predictor while deferring when labeled support does not justify the change. A 370-parameter operator combines source/target-conditioned spectral updates with a query-specific attenuation head. Two branches each fit 64 target labels and verify on the disjoint 64, then average their predictions. Training uses a smooth verification surrogate; inference applies a fixed nominal verification rule. On 20 fresh synthetic development seeds and two widths, sign-flip log-loss improves by .04584 [.03453,.05715] over frozen and .03235 [.02008,.04463] over logistic regression with the same verification rule. All 80 ignorable-shift branch decisions reject adaptation, and the predeclared exploratory robustness gate passes. Removing the query head loses .01804 [.00820,.02787] on sign flips. However, superiority over a trained scalar-filter/query-head control is uncertain, and unverified full-support logistic remains more accurate on sign flips while harming ignorable cases. These unadjusted seed-level intervals support a conditional adaptation/rejection benefit, not universal dominance or a distribution-free guarantee. A further fixed 20-seed development replication supports query conditioning against a capacity-matched support-only head, but the joint architectural gate and a 78-episode native-transfer gate fail. A subsequent native episodic family-transfer pilot with three initializations and matched capacity also fails its development gate. A distinct architectural novelty claim remains open. Checkpoints, full predictions, prior negative results and a disclosed numerical-control recovery are retained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}
The primary candidate is a trained support-verified query operator. We ask whether it can retain useful missingness corrections while rejecting updates that fail to generalize beyond their fitting labels. The present positive evidence is controlled synthetic development with a frozen logistic backbone; subsequent frozen-checkpoint native transfer fails its descriptive gate, and foundation-model confirmation is absent. Missingness adaptation and validation-guided meta-learning have established precedents\cite{zhou2023,rockenschaub2024,ren2018,metaweight}; a combination of these ingredients alone does not establish novelty. Earlier compiler, mixture, representation and native-data experiments are retained as supporting evidence and failure analysis, not as validation of this new operator.

\section{Support-verified query correction}
Source support contains 128 labels. Target support is split by fixed even/odd indices into two 64-label sets. Each branch fits the six-step spectral correction on one set and verifies the complete candidate on the other; roles are then reversed. The 257-parameter mode filter is supplemented with a 113-parameter query head. Its inputs are the frozen logit, bounded signed correction, correction magnitude, curvature score and source/target residual-mean difference. It produces a probability mixture of the correction and frozen predictor. All context statistics use labeled support only. The value--mask interaction basis is handcrafted. No query label enters fitting, query features or verification.

For verification examples define paired improvement $d_i=\ell(p_0,y_i)-\ell(p_\theta,y_i)$. The branch is accepted when $\bar d>1.645s_d/\sqrt{64}$, otherwise it returns $p_0$. Branch probabilities are averaged. This is a fixed nominal rule, not a finite-sample or simultaneous safety guarantee; the branches are dependent because support roles are reversed. During meta-training, query BCE receives equal weight from an unguarded ensemble and a smooth surrogate with acceptance weight $\sigma((\bar d-1.645\widehat{\mathrm{SE}})/(\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}+.005))$. The training/inference difference requires a later ablation. The inner convex-objective descent bound applies to the fitting solver, not the final mixture or query risk.

Three models receive 500 CUDA updates with identical episodes and batch schedules: the 370-parameter query operator,257-parameter no-query-head operator, and114-parameter scalar-filter/query-head control. Ordinary interaction logistic receives the same fitting/verification split and rule. A capacity-matched task-conditioned scalar control is reported below; the completed support-only-head control and fixed replication are reported next. Development uses20 fresh latent seeds, widths6/10 and four regimes; widths average within seed before unadjusted95\% t intervals. The separate confirmation seeds are untouched.

\section{Information equivalence}
Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ denote complete covariates, $Y\in\{0,1\}$ the outcome, and $M\in\{0,1\}^{d}$ a mask, with $M_j=1$ indicating an absent value. Write $\widetilde X$ for the table row in which missing entries carry a distinguished missing-value marker. The raw-input observation is $O=\widetilde X$, and $M=h(O)$ is measurable from it. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 P(Y\mid O,M)=P(Y\mid O).
 \label{eq:information}
\end{equation}
This elementary identity is not a new theorem. It says that appending the true mask adds no information to a fully retained incomplete input. It does not require a finite predictor to produce identical outputs.

For a labeled context $C$, compare a frozen predictor $F$ on native input and on the augmented representation:
\begin{align}
 p_{\rm nat} &= F(C,O),\\
 p_{\rm ind} &= F(C^{+},[O,M]).
\end{align}
Here $C^{+}$ contains the same context rows and labels with their masks appended. Augmentation changes feature count and potentially feature grouping, embeddings, and preprocessing. Mean imputation may obscure a native marker internally; explicit columns may then restore accessible information. A performance difference alone cannot identify a pretraining-prior explanation, and comparing checkpoints is not a controlled intervention on that prior.

\section{Trained reversible mask compiler}
For four selected mask bits, let $\mathcal A$ contain twelve binary matrices, each satisfying $A^2=I$ over $\mathbb F_2$. For a nonzero parity vector $a$, choose a pivot $i$ with $a_i=1$, replace row $i$ of $I$ by $a$, and retain the other identity rows. Single-bit vectors collapse to the identity, yielding twelve distinct matrices. The compiled coordinate vector is $z=Am\pmod 2$, with inverse $m=Az$. We append these coordinates to the unchanged incomplete table. On wider tables, only four support-selected mask columns are transformed; all remaining mask indicators are retained. Reversibility preserves statistical information and supplies an implementation check, not a new theorem or a guarantee about finite-predictor loss.

The trained prototype uses fifteen absolute support parity/label moments, one observed-variable/label moment and a context-size descriptor. A $17\to64\to64\to12$ ReLU network selects a basis. If its maximum softmax probability is below 0.8, it chooses the identity basis. This confidence rule is fixed and uncalibrated; it does not guarantee absence of harm. At inference the selector receives labeled support only, with no query outcomes, generator parameters or oracle posterior.

We train on 2,048 synthetic support tasks, with separate 256-task validation and 256-task development sets, using Adam at learning rate 0.003, batch size 128 and 80 fixed epochs. Privileged active-parity and zero-signal metadata define pretraining teachers only. The teacher identifies a basis, not the basis minimizing frozen-backbone query loss. Final epochs and confidence thresholds are fixed rather than selected on test outcomes. The prototype achieves 82.42\% raw and 78.12\% guarded teacher recovery on held-out development tasks; the fixed moment heuristic also achieves 78.12\%. These are teacher-recovery statistics, not downstream efficacy.

Matched competitors receive the same tasks, teachers, optimizer settings and exact minibatches: a 216-parameter linear selector and a 6,092-parameter raw-row Deep Sets encoder. Exact CPU replay reproduces the prototype's six weight arrays and all 1,280 updates. Equal epochs and parameter counts do not ensure equal optimization suitability: the prototype already receives engineered parity moments, whereas the row encoder must learn interactions. Support-CV basis search requires 108 logistic fits; the full-parity L1 comparator has fifteen mask features rather than four. We retain these feature and computation differences.

\section{Predictive-loss context adapter: development gate}
To replace privileged teacher recovery with a predictive objective, we train a separate 11,176-parameter context adapter. A shared row encoder pools labeled source and target support; learned pooled features, observed means and centered residual moments condition a hypernetwork producing query correction coefficients. Query features contain an always-observed scalar $U$, four incompletely observed auxiliary values, fifteen declared mask parities and a frozen source-predictor logit. The frozen predictor is a logistic regression fitted to source $U$ only, rather than a tabular foundation model. The adapter minimizes empirical query cross-entropy during meta-training; evaluation query labels, true policy parameters and oracle probabilities never enter inference.

Each outcome world fixes $P(Y\mid U)=\sigma(\beta U+b)$ while observation policies vary. A sixteen-pattern normalized mask law contains label-dependent parity, rate, correlated-block and value-dependent terms. Intercepts vary across worlds, but remain fixed within paired policies. The model sees 128 source labels and 64 target support labels. Meta-training uses 768 worlds with two policy cells each, 40 fixed epochs, and final-epoch checkpoints; 64 separate validation worlds are diagnostic only. An ordinary residual MLP has 12,601 parameters, while the identical context architecture trained without policy shifts has 11,176. Approximate capacity and equal update counts do not establish optimization fairness.

The frozen development protocol evaluates 48 new worlds with five policy cells each: unchanged, reversal, extreme rate, stronger correlated acquisition, and null. Cells share latent target rows within worlds, so averaging precedes task-level uncertainty. Only rate/block parameters exceed training support; reversal is generally a recombination of seen policies, and 17 of the 48 source signals are null. All nine methods' probabilities are retained across 240 episodes (2,160 prediction arrays). \section{Learned uncertainty mixtures and native CUDA training}
The failed dense correction motivates a separate finite-support uncertainty hypothesis. Sixteen candidate probabilities comprise frozen prediction and fifteen support-fitted single-parity corrections with fixed ridge regularization. Three-fold out-of-support losses, residual moments, source agreement and fitted coefficients provide twelve candidate-context features. A shared neural scorer with widths 32 and 16 has 961 parameters and assigns predictive mixture weights:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat p(y=1\mid q,C)=\sum_{j=0}^{15}\operatorname{softmax}_{j}(h_\theta(c_j))p_j(q).
\end{equation}
A fixed offset favors the frozen candidate at initialization. These are learned predictive weights, not a calibrated Bayesian mechanism posterior. Candidate probabilities depend on observed query inputs; fitting and context use labeled support only. A linear scorer receives identical candidate contexts, synthetic query labels and updates. \subsection{Learned spectral adaptation}
A subsequent257-parameter trained filter learns which source/target support-error eigenmodes to update. With fixed declared value--mask features, let $L(w)$ be offset logistic support loss plus $.05\|w\|^2/2$. Its Hessian is bounded by $H=.25A^\top A/n+.05I=V\operatorname{diag}(h)V^\top$. A learned gate $a_i\in[0,1]$ gives $B=V\operatorname{diag}(a_i/h_i)V^\top$ and $w'=w-B\nabla L(w)$. The inner basis is handcrafted; the shared error-mode filter is learned. Differentiable optimization and learned optimizers with guarantees have established precedents\cite{metaoptnet,learnedopt,pacopt}; novelty of this particular missingness use remains unresolved.

\section{Controlled data and oracle}
The initial synthetic diagnostic keeps an observed variable $U\in\{-1,+1\}$ balanced and uses
\begin{equation}
 P(Y=1\mid U)=\sigma(0.8U),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function. Independently generated Gaussian nuisance features receive masks with an outcome-associated mechanism. For a nonempty subset $A$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$, define
\begin{align}
 f_r(m,u)&=s u^{b}\prod_{j\in A}\frac{m_j-r}{\max(r,1-r)},\\
 P(M=m\mid Y=y,U=u)&=B_r(m)\bigl[1+\gamma(2y-1)f_r(m,u)\bigr],
 \label{eq:mechanism}
\end{align}
where $B_r$ is the product Bernoulli distribution with baseline rate $r\in(0,1)$, $s\in\{-1,+1\}$ and $0\leq\gamma<1$. Each centered factor has baseline mean zero and absolute value at most one, making the law normalized and nonnegative. At $r=0.5$ it equals the original balanced-mask diagnostic. The baseline rate need not equal each marginal rate after modulation. Its exact posterior is
\begin{equation}
 q^{\star}(u,m)=\sigma\left(0.8u+
 \log\frac{1+\gamma f_r(m,u)}{1-\gamma f_r(m,u)}\right).
 \label{eq:oracle}
\end{equation}
The mechanism supplies single-mask, pairwise, and value-dependent cases without multiplying a potentially mask-aware model posterior by an additional likelihood. At $\gamma=0$, the mask has no additional outcome signal given $U$. Holding the complete-data distribution fixed while changing the mechanism isolates the observation process. Additional value controls replace a fraction of nuisance entries by zero or round them to a fixed grid using independent random streams; labels, masks, and exact posteriors remain paired. Changing $r$ also changes signal strength, so equal $\gamma$ across rates is not equal oracle headroom.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
The first inference smoke test uses one accessible checkpoint, two signal strengths, and native versus augmented inputs to measure throughput. Shuffling preserves per-column missingness rates while disrupting row correspondence, and never uses labels. A follow-up compares context-mean-imputed inputs with and without true indicators. Transform parameters are fitted on context rows only; all-missing columns are retained with a documented fallback. Context and query identifiers are disjoint.

\end{equation}
where positive $G$ favors indicators. Synthetic expected log-loss uses the exact query probability:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

All 360 requested cells completed. The primary native-to-indicator gain for TabPFN v2 at $\gamma=0.9$ is 0.10389 nats [0.07969, 0.12809]; the true-versus-shuffled indicator gain is 0.09499 [0.06162, 0.12837]. Coverage is nominal and relies on the task-level $t$-interval assumptions. These are the prespecified 97.5\% intervals for the two primary contrasts; both meet the frozen success rule. The TabPFN zero-signal native-to-indicator effect is $-0.00254$ [$-0.00545$, 0.00038] nats; its descriptive interval includes zero. \section{Strong operational baselines}
After the frozen confirmation, a descriptive follow-up evaluates six CPU methods on its forty saved tasks, producing 240 saved predictions. Each operational method uses the identical 256 support labels and query observations. Regularization is selected by support-only three-fold cross-validation and the final predictor refits the full context. The interaction logistic models use $U$, all individual masks, all mask pairs and their interactions with $U$; they do not receive the generator's active columns, sign, true signal strength or query targets. Query targets and oracle probabilities enter scoring only after predictions are saved. A native-value/indicator histogram boosting baseline supplies a generic nonlinear comparator.

At $\gamma=0.9$, the L1 interaction logistic model has expected loss 0.19336, only 0.00893 above the exact oracle. It descriptively improves over indicator-augmented TabPFN v2 by 0.01595 [0.00662, 0.02527] nats and over TabICLv2 by 0.01053 [0.00357, 0.01750]. These follow-up intervals are unadjusted and do not constitute a new blind confirmation. The fitted-base sparse mixture estimates its base probabilities using a support-fitted $U$ logistic model and uses a declared sparse mask-term/gamma prior. It does not receive the true active term or gamma. Table~\ref{tab:strong} summarizes the operational comparisons.

\section{Frozen real-covariate panel}
All six declared datasets and thirty grouped outer folds completed, producing 1,440 cells under the committed protocol. Every predictor receives the same 128 support labels, held-out queries and realized observation masks within a condition. No datasets were replaced or models tuned after panel scores. Independent reconstruction verifies all 120 episodes, input/source identities, mask seeds and distributions, saved probability row identities, empirical log-loss, Brier scores and AUROC. All 48 paired dataset-level contrasts and six-unit intervals reproduce to numerical precision; no fit/predict warnings were recorded.

Table~\ref{tab:realpanel} shows every native-to-indicator panel contrast, including negative results. At $\gamma=0.8$, TabPFN v2 gains 0.00964 nats at $r=0.1$ and 0.00382 at $r=0.5$; both six-dataset intervals include zero. TabICLv2 effects are near zero or negative. These outcomes provide no clear dataset-balanced TFM gain on this fixed single-active-mask panel, while leaving uncertainty about smaller effects and untested settings. They do not refute the confirmed pairwise synthetic result: the observation mechanism, covariates and label budget differ.

Generic logistic regression uses mean-imputed covariates, so its explicit indicators can restore distinctions obscured by that transformation. At $r=0.5,\gamma=0.8$, its gain is 0.17141 [0.07418,0.26863] nats, but at $\gamma=0$ the same augmentation harms it by 0.03373 [0.00286,0.06461]. This is a finite-label tradeoff under imposed label association, not evidence for a neural adapter. XGBoost's native and true-indicator probabilities are bit-identical throughout; shuffled indicators can change its predictions. Full shuffled contrasts remain reported. At $r=0.1,\gamma=0.8$, TabICLv2's true-versus-shuffled gain is 0.01333 [0.00228,0.02437], despite its native-to-indicator interval including zero. A shuffled-view advantage alone therefore does not establish improvement over native inputs.

A support-fitted SimpleImputer reference finds no observed active-column values equal to its fitted mean in either support or query rows, and no context-constant active column across all six datasets. Ionosphere has an inactive constant column; Spambase has rare inactive columns constant in some contexts. This panel therefore does not reproduce the deliberate informative-mask erasure in the all-zero diagnostic. The calculation uses saved float32 inputs and the declared imputer; it does not instrument every downstream model operation.

The labels used to impose retrospective missingness are evaluator-only. Class-conditional probabilities on the active column are 0.02/0.18 at $r=0.1$ and 0.1/0.9 at $r=0.5$ when $\gamma=0.8$; realized rates and all original/imposed masks are retained. Native missingness is absent. Unknown acquisition groups, numerical feature selection, small context budgets, six nonrepresentative datasets and overlapping training folds limit interpretation. This is controlled evaluation on real covariates, not validation of a natural observation policy.

\section{Related work and limitations}
Learning to recommend feature transformations from dataset summaries is established~\cite{nargesian}; permutation-invariant set encoders~\cite{deepsets} and learned nonlinear missingness processing~\cite{neumiss} are also prior art. The candidate combination here is support-conditioned selection of an invertible mask basis for a frozen predictor. Parity features, reversibility, an MLP and privileged synthetic training individually do not establish novelty. The prototype's exact tie with the heuristic on signal tasks directly limits a learned-method claim.
TabPFN v2 demonstrates strong small-data prediction~\cite{hollmann2025}. Contemporary versions already encode missingness: TabPFN-3.5 explicitly inherits missing and infiniteness indicators~\cite{priorlabs2026}. Thus, native indicator encoding is compatible with our question, and a blanket claim that foundation models discard masks would be incorrect.

PFN-Flow studies Bayesian prediction under structural missingness, with guarantees restricted to its specified prior~\cite{liang2026}. TFM-Retouche uses a lightweight residual input adapter through a frozen backbone and a held-out identity guard~\cite{nguyen2026}. These precedents prevent treating missingness-specific training or frozen residual adaptation alone as methodological novelty.

The specified pairwise TabPFN contrast has fresh-task confirmation; other synthetic families remain exploratory. The completed six-dataset imposed-missingness panel gives small, uncertain TFM gains rather than evidence of a general native-input limitation. The CPU pilot is narrow, high-signal, and synthetic; its Monte Carlo variability does not describe generalization across real datasets. The zero-collision condition is deliberately extreme. A representation effect may disappear on current models or follow feature-count changes. TabPFN v3.5 inference was inaccessible because one-time license acceptance and a token were required; no checkpoint was substituted. Causal attribution to pretraining requires matched controlled training beyond this protocol. The current support-verified operator has a completed capacity-matched support-only-head contrast supporting query conditioning on synthetic data, but its native-transfer gate fails. Its completed capacity-matched scalar control does not establish a sign-flip advantage for mode-specific learning. Its synthetic verification rule is nominal, branch outcomes are dependent, and development confidence intervals do not establish distribution-free safety. The positive query-head contrast must not be generalized to every architectural component. Any online extension must predict before label release and retain original probabilities for scoring. This draft makes no acceptance, clinical, or universal-improvement claim.
