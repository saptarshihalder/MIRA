\begin{abstract}
We train MIRA-Compiler, a 6,092-parameter support-conditioned selector of reversible missingness coordinates for a frozen tabular predictor. Synthetic pretraining uses privileged basis teachers; inference receives labeled support only. On 512 new development tasks, its predictions match a fixed moment heuristic on all 256 informative tasks. A hash-frozen 324-prediction backbone screen finds expected log-loss gains over uncompiled indicators of 0.086 nats for TabPFN v2 and 0.061 for TabICLv2, averaging pairwise and four-way tasks within three seed units. The heuristic reproduces all synthetic compiler predictions, so these improvements do not establish a learned-method advantage. On Hepatitis and Horse Colic, the compiler is identical to uncompiled indicators; augmentation helps or harms depending on dataset. An earlier twenty-task confirmation establishes a narrower TabPFN representation effect, with gain 0.104 nats over native inputs; strong interaction logistic baselines nearly close its oracle gap. We retain the trained candidate, source/checkpoint identities, controls and negative results, but the useful learned-contribution gate is not met. Current-checkpoint transfer, broad natural-missingness benefits and venue readiness remain unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}
Our primary candidate is MIRA-Compiler, a trained selector of reversible missingness coordinates for a frozen predictor. Its purpose is to expose a task-relevant mask interaction as an individual coordinate while retaining the raw incomplete table. Training alone does not establish a useful contribution: our development screen compares the learned selector with fixed moments, equally pretrained competitors, support-only basis search and uncompiled inputs. We report failures of this screen as well as improvements. The earlier representation experiments establish motivation rather than evidence of learned-model superiority.

Tabular foundation models predict from labeled examples without updating their pretrained weights. Their treatment of missing data depends on architecture, preprocessing, and inference configuration. We ask whether representing missingness as explicit binary columns changes prediction when the raw statistical information is unchanged. An affirmative result would establish a useful behavioral limitation or opportunity, with scope determined by the tested models and mechanisms.

The question concerns representation rather than imputation accuracy. It also differs from claiming that every missingness shift changes the optimal predictor. Previous work distinguishes ignorable shifts from non-ignorable changes that can alter the Bayes predictor~\cite{rockenschaub2024}. Observation-policy adaptation is already an established problem~\cite{zhou2023}. These precedents constrain novelty claims and motivate direct evaluation of the trained compiler against simple corrections.

\section{Information equivalence}
Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ denote complete covariates, $Y\in\{0,1\}$ the outcome, and $M\in\{0,1\}^{d}$ a mask, with $M_j=1$ indicating an absent value. Write $\widetilde X$ for the table row in which missing entries carry a distinguished missing-value marker. The raw-input observation is $O=\widetilde X$, and $M=h(O)$ is measurable from it. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 P(Y\mid O,M)=P(Y\mid O).
 \label{eq:information}
\end{equation}
This elementary identity is not a new theorem. It says that appending the true mask adds no information to a fully retained incomplete input. It does not require a finite predictor to produce identical outputs.

For a labeled context $C$, compare a frozen predictor $F$ on native input and on the augmented representation:
\begin{align}
 p_{\rm nat} &= F(C,O),\\
 p_{\rm ind} &= F(C^{+},[O,M]).
\end{align}
Here $C^{+}$ contains the same context rows and labels with their masks appended. Augmentation changes feature count and potentially feature grouping, embeddings, and preprocessing. Mean imputation may obscure a native marker internally; explicit columns may then restore accessible information. A performance difference alone cannot identify a pretraining-prior explanation, and comparing checkpoints is not a controlled intervention on that prior.

\section{Trained reversible mask compiler}
For four selected mask bits, let $\mathcal A$ contain twelve binary matrices, each satisfying $A^2=I$ over $\mathbb F_2$. For a nonzero parity vector $a$, choose a pivot $i$ with $a_i=1$, replace row $i$ of $I$ by $a$, and retain the other identity rows. Single-bit vectors collapse to the identity, yielding twelve distinct matrices. The compiled coordinate vector is $z=Am\pmod 2$, with inverse $m=Az$. We append these coordinates to the unchanged incomplete table. On wider tables, only four support-selected mask columns are transformed; all remaining mask indicators are retained. Reversibility preserves statistical information and supplies an implementation check, not a new theorem or a guarantee about finite-predictor loss.

The trained prototype uses fifteen absolute support parity/label moments, one observed-variable/label moment and a context-size descriptor. A $17\to64\to64\to12$ ReLU network selects a basis. If its maximum softmax probability is below 0.8, it chooses the identity basis. This confidence rule is fixed and uncalibrated; it does not guarantee absence of harm. At inference the selector receives labeled support only, with no query outcomes, generator parameters or oracle posterior.

We train on 2,048 synthetic support tasks, with separate 256-task validation and 256-task development sets, using Adam at learning rate 0.003, batch size 128 and 80 fixed epochs. Privileged active-parity and zero-signal metadata define pretraining teachers only. The teacher identifies a basis, not the basis minimizing frozen-backbone query loss. Final epochs and confidence thresholds are fixed rather than selected on test outcomes. The prototype achieves 82.42\% raw and 78.12\% guarded teacher recovery on held-out development tasks; the fixed moment heuristic also achieves 78.12\%. These are teacher-recovery statistics, not downstream efficacy.

Matched competitors receive the same tasks, teachers, optimizer settings and exact minibatches: a 216-parameter linear selector and a 6,092-parameter raw-row Deep Sets encoder. Exact CPU replay reproduces the prototype's six weight arrays and all 1,280 updates. Equal epochs and parameter counts do not ensure equal optimization suitability: the prototype already receives engineered parity moments, whereas the row encoder must learn interactions. Support-CV basis search requires 108 logistic fits; the full-parity L1 comparator has fifteen mask features rather than four. We retain these feature and computation differences.

\section{Naturally missing retrospective outcomes}
We retain native missing values in UCI Hepatitis~\cite{hepatitis} and Horse Colic~\cite{horse}, with 155 and 353 canonical rows respectively. Official CSV entries are checked against the original archives, with cached source hashes and CC BY 4.0 attribution. Horse Colic excludes two unknown outcomes, thirteen exact retained-feature/label duplicates and outcome, treatment, lesion and identifier fields from predictor inputs. Death and euthanasia form the positive outcome. Original train/test records are pooled before three grouped folds; supplied hospital identifiers and identical covariates define isolated groups. This cannot establish independence of unknown patients or prospective availability of measurements.

All retained covariates and native NaNs reach each backbone. The selector uses the four columns with highest support missing rates, with stable label-free tie-breaking. Its observed-variable proxy is zero and is never added as a backbone covariate. Nominal category codes are treated numerically, a limitation shared by these comparisons. Three folds within each dataset share training rows; we aggregate query-weighted folds within dataset and report two descriptive dataset results rather than six independent replications.

Twenty-four full-feature CPU control predictions are complete, without recorded fit/predict warnings. Native-to-identity-indicator logistic losses are 0.46155 to 0.40453 on Hepatitis and 0.55561 to 0.56094 on Horse Colic. Fixed histogram boosting is unchanged at 0.56303 and 0.75336. \section{Predictive-loss context adapter: development gate}
To replace privileged teacher recovery with a predictive objective, we train a separate 11,176-parameter context adapter. A shared row encoder pools labeled source and target support; learned pooled features, observed means and centered residual moments condition a hypernetwork producing query correction coefficients. Query features contain an always-observed scalar $U$, four incompletely observed auxiliary values, fifteen declared mask parities and a frozen source-predictor logit. The frozen predictor is a logistic regression fitted to source $U$ only, rather than a tabular foundation model. The adapter minimizes empirical query cross-entropy during meta-training; evaluation query labels, true policy parameters and oracle probabilities never enter inference.

Each outcome world fixes $P(Y\mid U)=\sigma(\beta U+b)$ while observation policies vary. A sixteen-pattern normalized mask law contains label-dependent parity, rate, correlated-block and value-dependent terms. Intercepts vary across worlds, but remain fixed within paired policies. The model sees 128 source labels and 64 target support labels. Meta-training uses 768 worlds with two policy cells each, 40 fixed epochs, and final-epoch checkpoints; 64 separate validation worlds are diagnostic only. An ordinary residual MLP has 12,601 parameters, while the identical context architecture trained without policy shifts has 11,176. Approximate capacity and equal update counts do not establish optimization fairness.

The frozen development protocol evaluates 48 new worlds with five policy cells each: unchanged, reversal, extreme rate, stronger correlated acquisition, and null. Cells share latent target rows within worlds, so averaging precedes task-level uncertainty. Only rate/block parameters exceed training support; reversal is generally a recombination of seen policies, and 17 of the 48 source signals are null. All nine methods' probabilities are retained across 240 episodes (2,160 prediction arrays). Table~\ref{tab:policy-context} reports equally weighted expected NLL, using an exact generative posterior solely for scoring.

\section{Controlled data and oracle}
The initial synthetic diagnostic keeps an observed variable $U\in\{-1,+1\}$ balanced and uses
\begin{equation}
 P(Y=1\mid U)=\sigma(0.8U),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function. Independently generated Gaussian nuisance features receive masks with an outcome-associated mechanism. For a nonempty subset $A$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$, define
\begin{align}
 f_r(m,u)&=s u^{b}\prod_{j\in A}\frac{m_j-r}{\max(r,1-r)},\\
 P(M=m\mid Y=y,U=u)&=B_r(m)\bigl[1+\gamma(2y-1)f_r(m,u)\bigr],
 \label{eq:mechanism}
\end{align}
where $B_r$ is the product Bernoulli distribution with baseline rate $r\in(0,1)$, $s\in\{-1,+1\}$ and $0\leq\gamma<1$. Each centered factor has baseline mean zero and absolute value at most one, making the law normalized and nonnegative. At $r=0.5$ it equals the original balanced-mask diagnostic. The baseline rate need not equal each marginal rate after modulation. Its exact posterior is
\begin{equation}
 q^{\star}(u,m)=\sigma\left(0.8u+
 \log\frac{1+\gamma f_r(m,u)}{1-\gamma f_r(m,u)}\right).
 \label{eq:oracle}
\end{equation}
The mechanism supplies single-mask, pairwise, and value-dependent cases without multiplying a potentially mask-aware model posterior by an additional likelihood. At $\gamma=0$, the mask has no additional outcome signal given $U$. Holding the complete-data distribution fixed while changing the mechanism isolates the observation process. Additional value controls replace a fraction of nuisance entries by zero or round them to a fixed grid using independent random streams; labels, masks, and exact posteriors remain paired. Changing $r$ also changes signal strength, so equal $\gamma$ across rates is not equal oracle headroom.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
The first inference smoke test uses one accessible checkpoint, two signal strengths, and native versus augmented inputs to measure throughput. The development matrix then considers $\gamma\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.9\}$, 256 context rows, 1,024 query rows, and three independent draws. Four ensemble members are the initial setting; any change must be recorded before confirmation.

Each draw compares native incomplete inputs, native inputs with true indicators, and native inputs with independently shuffled indicator columns of identical width. Shuffling preserves per-column missingness rates while disrupting row correspondence, and never uses labels. A follow-up compares context-mean-imputed inputs with and without true indicators. Transform parameters are fitted on context rows only; all-missing columns are retained with a documented fallback. Context and query identifiers are disjoint.

The backbone panel begins with explicitly pinned TabPFN v2 and TabICLv2. A current mask-aware TabPFN checkpoint is included if access and runtime permit. Native-NaN tree models provide an ordinary supervised comparison. Package versions, checkpoint filenames and hashes, ensemble settings, random seeds, preprocessing, elapsed time, and billed resources accompany every run. A blocked checkpoint is reported rather than silently replaced.

The primary paired effect is
\begin{equation}
 G=L_{\rm nat}-L_{\rm ind},
\end{equation}
where positive $G$ favors indicators. Synthetic expected log-loss uses the exact query probability:
\begin{equation}
 L(p)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
 \left[-q_i^{\star}\log p_i-(1-q_i^{\star})\log(1-p_i)\right].
\end{equation}
We report raw losses, oracle headroom, and task-level uncertainty. A headroom fraction is reported only when its denominator exceeds $10^{-4}$; negative effects remain visible. Queries within a task do not become independent experimental replicates. The first confirmation protocol was committed before evaluation and used twenty fresh draws, seeds 60000--60019. Two prespecified TabPFN v2 contrasts at $\gamma=0.9$ use 97.5\% two-sided paired $t$ intervals, giving nominal 95\% simultaneous coverage by Bonferroni. Success requires both lower bounds positive and a native-to-indicator mean gain of at least 0.01 nats. Gamma-zero and other backbones are descriptive controls. No outcome-based stopping or selection was used.

Follow-ups vary label budgets, missingness rates, interactions, and values near imputation constants. The real-covariate protocol fixes six public UCI binary datasets: Wisconsin Diagnostic, Ionosphere, Banknote Authentication, Spambase, Sonar, and Statlog Heart~\cite{wdbc,ionosphere,banknote,spambase,sonar,heart}. Heart retains five continuous features. Exact $(X,y)$ duplicates are removed with multiplicities retained; identical feature vectors, including conflicting labels, share groups. Provided source identifiers also constrain grouping and are excluded from predictors. The resulting 6,955 rows enter five group-isolated outer folds per dataset. Each fold uses exactly 128 stratified support labels and at most 1,024 held-out queries. These are within-dataset tests; unavailable acquisition groups remain a limitation.

Native masks are verified absent in these datasets. We separately save an imposed mask with baseline rate $r\in\{0.1,0.5\}$. One active column is chosen per fold by seeded randomness without inspecting performance; its missing probability is $r[1+\gamma(2y-1)]$, for $\gamma\in\{0,0.8\}$, while other columns have rate $r$. This retrospective intervention uses query labels only in the evaluator's mask generator. The predictor receives the resulting observation, never query targets or the active-column identity. Shared uniform streams pair conditions; original values, native masks, imposed masks, and row/group identities remain saved.

The panel compares TabPFN v2, TabICLv2, XGBoost and generic imputed/scaled logistic regression under the same three representations. Logistic regularization is chosen inside a support-only three-fold stratified group CV pipeline. The complete protocol, runner source and input hashes were committed before any panel scores. Empirical clipped log-loss, Brier score and AUROC are retained; there is no real-data oracle. Within each dataset we average paired fold losses using query counts, then give each of the six datasets equal weight. Nominal unadjusted 95\% $t$ intervals use six dataset effects, not thirty overlapping folds or individual rows. This fixed, nonrepresentative panel cannot establish universal or natural-missingness performance.

\subsection{Value and imputation controls}
Additional development controls use three new task seeds per condition, $\gamma\in\{0,0.5,0.9\}$, and the same context/query and ensemble budgets. Partial collisions set independently selected fractions 0.1 or 0.5 of observed nuisance values to zero. A quantized control rounds Gaussian values to a unit grid; these three value conditions have identical $U,Y,M$, oracles, and row identifiers. At $\gamma=0.9$, TabICLv2's native-to-indicator effects are 0.00066 [$-0.00376$, 0.00507], 0.00111 [$-0.00544$, 0.00766], and 0.00341 [0.00245, 0.00436] nats respectively. The large all-zero failure therefore does not generalize to these partial collisions; the small quantized effect needs independent confirmation. TabPFN v2's effects are 0.01711, 0.02152, and 0.01697, with all three intervals including zero. XGBoost's native and indicator predictions coincide.

Matched context-only imputation controls make the information distinction explicit. At $\gamma=0.9$ with all-zero observed nuisance values, adding indicators after mean imputation improves expected loss by 0.45021 [0.43058, 0.46984] for TabICLv2, 0.44677 [0.43428, 0.45926] for TabPFN v2, and 0.42049 [0.39539, 0.44559] for XGBoost. This is ordinary information restoration after a lossy transformation, whereas the original native marker already identifies the mask. TabICLv2's all-zero native and explicitly imputed predictions coincide exactly in all nine matched tasks. Gaussian imputed-input gains at $\gamma=0.9$ have intervals including zero for all three models. A separate stress control appends sixteen independently shuffled columns; it differs in width from the eight true indicators and is not an exact feature-count isolation. Its all-zero effects at $\gamma=0.9$ all have intervals including zero. At $\gamma=0$, all-zero imputed indicators harm XGBoost by 0.0835 nats on average, underscoring that retained information does not guarantee a finite-sample gain.

This reference calculation does not instrument the model's internal floating-point preprocessing. These controls test zero mass and discretization, not a calibrated dose of exact mean collisions.

These development results motivate a frozen interaction-focused confirmation design; selection and multiplicity prevent interpreting these intervals as confirmatory evidence.

A further baseline-rate-$0.1$ panel contains 243 cells over single-mask, pairwise, and value-dependent families. At $\gamma=0.9$, TabPFN native-to-indicator effects are 0.03329 [0.00697, 0.05960], $-0.00006$ [$-0.00212$, 0.00200], and 0.03935 [0.02040, 0.05831]; TabICL value-dependent gain is 0.04167 [0.00894, 0.07439]. Equal signal-strength parameters across rates do not give equal oracle headroom. Across the original and expanded panels, 1,296 development cells completed; twelve engineering smoke cells are excluded. Sixty local tests and a saved-data audit pass. At the end of development, confirmation remained untouched.

\section{Related work and limitations}
Learning to recommend feature transformations from dataset summaries is established~\cite{nargesian}; permutation-invariant set encoders~\cite{deepsets} and learned nonlinear missingness processing~\cite{neumiss} are also prior art. The candidate combination here is support-conditioned selection of an invertible mask basis for a frozen predictor. Parity features, reversibility, an MLP and privileged synthetic training individually do not establish novelty. The prototype's exact tie with the heuristic on signal tasks directly limits a learned-method claim.
TabPFN v2 demonstrates strong small-data prediction~\cite{hollmann2025}. Contemporary versions already encode missingness: TabPFN-3.5 explicitly inherits missing and infiniteness indicators~\cite{priorlabs2026}. Thus, native indicator encoding is compatible with our question, and a blanket claim that foundation models discard masks would be incorrect.

PFN-Flow studies Bayesian prediction under structural missingness, with guarantees restricted to its specified prior~\cite{liang2026}. TFM-Retouche uses a lightweight residual input adapter through a frozen backbone and a held-out identity guard~\cite{nguyen2026}. These precedents prevent treating missingness-specific training or frozen residual adaptation alone as methodological novelty.

The specified pairwise TabPFN contrast has fresh-task confirmation; other synthetic families remain exploratory. The completed six-dataset imposed-missingness panel gives small, uncertain TFM gains rather than evidence of a general native-input limitation. The CPU pilot is narrow, high-signal, and synthetic; its Monte Carlo variability does not describe generalization across real datasets. The zero-collision condition is deliberately extreme. A representation effect may disappear on current models or follow feature-count changes. TabPFN v3.5 inference was inaccessible because one-time license acceptance and a token were required; no checkpoint was substituted. Causal attribution to pretraining requires matched controlled training beyond this protocol. A neural branch requires held-out improvement beyond simple corrections and refreshed contexts with matched labels. Any online extension must predict before label release and retain original probabilities for scoring. This draft makes no acceptance, clinical, or universal-improvement claim.
