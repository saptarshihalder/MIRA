\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Lifted Gaussian information sites provide a concrete way to retain shared sensor noise when predictions must use changing subsets of observations. Their exact interpretation holds under the stated linear Gaussian assumptions. In the recorded synthetic experiments, a small learned cavity network improves on the tested support fitted methods and ordinary transformer recipe, including transfer from five to sixteen sensors. A lifted transformer readout preserves that benefit and improves on its matched ordinary head on new synthetic panels.

On real networks, combining a flexible transformer with the site readout recovers part of the gap left by the small cavity network. The conventional transformer retains an advantage in several conditions, and the declared noninferiority margin is not established. Together, the theory and experiments identify a useful design principle: retain common disturbances in the fusion state, and learn from calibration data how each observed sensor should modify that state.
